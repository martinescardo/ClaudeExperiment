# What this is

This is an experiment.

The background is that a number of people have been advocating to use AI to develop mathematics, and that I was accused by peers of dismissing this initiative, and, at the same time, attacked by other peers regarding even considering this.

So I took this challenge seriously, and decided to check what the latest advances in so-called AI could contribute to things I and my collaborators are thinking about and care about.

Further background is that Thierry Coquand performed two interesting experiments, recording the formalization of two previously known facts, with previously known paper proofs:

  1. T. Coquand, *Auto formalisation of Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem in Binary Recursive Arithmetic*, [arXiv:2606.01898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01898).
  1. T. Coquand, *Auto formalisation of Chaitin and of the surprise incompleteness Theorem*, [arXiv:2606.12462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12462).

What motivated me to do what is done here is whether AI's can actually "think" about open problems, and, at the same time, formalize them and their solutions or partial progress.

The results I got in my chosen problem are mixed, with both progress and walls. You will have to judge by yourself: see Claude's own report, [`an-ai-account.pdf`](../Claude/report/an-ai-account.pdf).

This is the **only** file written by [Martín Escardó](https://www.cs.bham.ac.uk/~mhe/) in this repository. Everything else was written by a so-called AI after he wrote more [prompts](../../Prompts.md) than he could cope with, to the extent he got bored, and wished that he was instead working with a PhD student, rather than a machine.

The author of this experiment thinks it is more fun and enlightening to interact with collaborators, including PhD students, than with so-called AI's.

But here we are, and two things happened to me:

  1. I still think it is more fun to work with people rather than machines.
  2. Machines were actually OK. But just OK. And also impressive, e.g. writing Agda code that we know we could have written ourselves if we wanted to and if we were patient enough.
